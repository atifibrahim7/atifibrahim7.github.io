RETRIEVAL ENGINE PRODUCTION SYSTEM

Black RAG

Retrieval that can say no.

A retrieval engine that re-ranks on more than similarity, and answers "I don't have this" instead of guessing.

Always answering
is the failure mode.

Standard vector search always returns its top-k nearest neighbours, whether or not the answer actually exists in the corpus. When it doesn't, the model gets handed irrelevant context and writes a confident answer on top of it anyway.

A RAG system that always answers, even off-corpus, is a hallucination generator by design. There is no mechanism for the retrieval layer to say "not found."

Three inspectable
stages.

Retrieve over-fetches candidates from the vector store, several times more than will actually be used, and re-ranks them on a blend of semantic similarity and lexical or geometric distance rather than semantic similarity alone. A chunk can be the best lexical match for a query, it literally contains the numbers or terms being asked about, while ranking poorly on pure semantic similarity, or the reverse. Relying on either signal alone misses real answers the other one would have caught.

Resonate takes that re-ranked list and applies an admission threshold. If nothing in the candidate set clears the bar, the system returns a typed null signal, a structured refusal that names why nothing was admitted (below threshold, no resonance at all, access denied, a version-visibility conflict), instead of forcing through a best-effort match.

Marshal sits in front as a router. Before conceding to a null signal, it runs an escalation ladder: progressively broader retrieval strategies attempted in sequence, each one logged, so giving up is the last resort rather than the first response to a hard query.

Every decision along this path, what was retrieved, why it was admitted or refused, which escalation step fired, is written to a SHA-256 hash-chained audit ledger, with full multi-tenant isolation between customers.

What makes it
different.

Typed refusal responses instead of forced top-k results: the system can say "not found," with a reason.

A four-level escalation ladder tries progressively broader retrieval strategies before giving up.

Hybrid re-ranking on semantic similarity and lexical geometry together, not similarity alone.

Full pipeline traceability: every retrieval decision is inspectable after the fact.

SHA-256 hash-chained audit ledger.

Multi-tenant isolation.

Measured, not
projected.

In an internal evaluation against a real public company's annual report, fixing table-aware ingestion and recalibrating the admission gate's ranking blend took live answer coverage on a fixed question set from roughly 1 of 37 to 19 of 37.

Table-based questions specifically went from 0 of 4 answered to 4 of 4.

The admission threshold was tuned from measured data: a lexical-only gate admitted only 19 of 33 answerable questions at zero false positives. The blended semantic and lexical gate admits 27 of 33 at that same zero-false-positive bar.

These are the project's own internal evaluation numbers, from a live eval harness against a real document, not projections from a design paper. No independently published ranking-quality benchmark exists, and none is claimed here.
