API GATEWAY / USAGE BILLING / AI AGENTS BACKEND SERVICE

Headless Horseman

Who gets in, and who pays.

A service that sits in front of any API or AI tool server. On every call it decides yes or no, writes a tamper-proof record, and bills from that same record. All in about 10 milliseconds.

AI agents call APIs
thousands of times a minute.

Someone has to decide which calls get through, count them, and charge for them. Most companies glue that together from three separate systems, and the three never quite agree.

The rate limiter says one thing, the usage counter says another, and the invoice says a third. When a customer disputes a bill, nobody can prove what really happened. With AI agents that spawn their own sub-agents, it gets worse: you also need to know who is calling on whose behalf, and with how much permission.

One record,
three jobs.

Every request goes through Horseman before it reaches your API. Horseman checks it against the customer's plan: speed limits, monthly quota, credit balance, and what the caller is allowed to do. Then it answers: let it in, or refuse it with a clear reason and when to try again.

Whatever it decides, it writes one event to a log that can only be added to, never edited. Each event is cryptographically signed, and the log is sealed in batches (Merkle checkpoints), so a separate offline tool can prove that nothing was changed after the fact.

Everything else is computed from that one log: usage meters, credit balances, invoices, and the answer to "why was I refused?". Because billing reads the same record as the gate, the bill cannot drift from what actually happened.

"Headless" means there is no screen. The service is the API. Dashboards, customer portals and SDKs are all just clients of it, with no private back door.

What makes it
different.

The gate, the meter and the bill read one signed log, so they always agree.

Tamper-proof by design: signed events plus checkpoints that an offline verifier can audit.

Agent-aware: it knows whether a person, an agent or a sub-agent is calling, and narrows permissions at each step down the chain.

Polite refusals: standard 429 responses with a reason and a retry time, so callers back off instead of hammering.

Pays its own way: real Stripe billing, prepaid credits, and pay-per-call for agents.

Where it stands.

~10 ms median time to decide a call.

~318 req/s held for 13 minutes with flat memory.

219 / 219 automated tests passing.

End-to-end runs against the real Stripe sandbox, not mocks.

Load figures come from my own test runs on a single process, not an independent benchmark.
